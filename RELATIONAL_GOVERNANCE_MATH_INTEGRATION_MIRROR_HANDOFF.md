## New mathematical constraints to integrate

1. Snapshot membership alone does not establish causal relation or continuity.
2. A causal transition is resolution-indexed; refinement may expose additional transitions without erasing the established coarse causal relation.
3. Admissibility is governance-indexed. Actual observation witnesses first-order realizability under operative reality; higher-order state manifolds may impose additional constraints.
4. Higher-order restriction does not rewrite causal history and does not automatically taint later transitions in the same trajectory.
5. Lineage contamination, decomposition-sensitive admissibility, and precedence are additional explicit governance relations, never implicit consequences of refinement or ordinal governance labels.
6. A classification has no enforcement effect without a causal intervention path that changes the target manifold's reachable response-transition set.
7. Time may be state/evidence consumed by governance but is not a primitive determinant of causal transition identity.

## Formalism mapping

### RTG

`Theta_rho : x -> y` is an established causal relation at resolution `rho`. A refinement map may factor `Theta_rho` into finer transition operators while preserving its coarse projection. RTG ledgers must distinguish transition identity, observation resolution, governance judgment, and optional lineage rules.

### TT

Transition blocks classify observed or candidate transitions. A black/restricted block may be followed by an independently admissible block. TT must not propagate restriction across the trajectory without an explicit lineage invariant.

### GTG

Reconstruction establishes causal/governance relationships from evidence. It must not substitute a higher-order admissibility result for causal existence, and must record the governor under which each admissibility statement is made.

### STCM

Moment/receipt relations bind observations and causal/governance evidence. Receipts do not manufacture causal identity, and clock coordinates are evidentiary unless explicitly made authoritative by a governance invariant.
