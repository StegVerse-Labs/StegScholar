# Relational Transition Geometry Claims Register

## Purpose

This register separates definitions, model-derived results, testable hypotheses, analogies, speculative extensions, and prohibited overclaims for Relational Transition Geometry (RTG).

## Status labels

- **DEFINITION:** introduced by the RTG formalism.
- **MODEL-DERIVED:** follows from declared assumptions but lacks independent validation.
- **TESTABLE HYPOTHESIS:** has a stated falsification path.
- **ESTABLISHED DOMAIN RESULT:** accepted in a bounded source domain and requiring primary-source citation.
- **ANALOGY:** structurally suggestive comparison without demonstrated equivalence.
- **SPECULATIVE EXTENSION:** research direction outside the initial validation package.
- **DISALLOWED OVERCLAIM:** prohibited without new derivation and evidence.

## Initial falsification obligations

RTG v0.1 must include:

1. a combined-signal case where source reconstruction succeeds;
2. a matched case where source reconstruction is provably or empirically non-identifiable;
3. a multi-ledger case where comparative evidence improves reconstruction;
4. a counterexample where additional ledgers do not resolve ambiguity;
5. a cross-scale chain with a deliberately omitted intermediate state;
6. a control case where noncommutativity is caused by a bad scale map rather than a missing transition.

## Publication boundary

The initial RTG paper may claim only that the formalism:

- defines world-regions, intersection translations, comparative ledgers, scale maps, and invariant-preservation tests;
- produces deterministic toy-model results;
- distinguishes reconstructability from physical persistence and observer accessibility;
- generates falsifiable cross-scale questions.

It must not conclude that RTG:

- solves quantum gravity;
- decodes black-hole information;
- establishes a universal physical scalar;
- proves a final ontology of identity;
- uniquely reconstructs every real transition;
- supersedes domain-specific physical theories.

## Update rule

Every material RTG manuscript claim must cite an identifier from this register. A status may advance only when its derivation, fixture, validator, dataset, primary literature, or independent review receipt is committed and version-bound.
