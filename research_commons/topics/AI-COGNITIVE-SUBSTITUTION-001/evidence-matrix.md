## Construct boundaries

### Artifact / learning quality
Whether the terminal essay, answer, proof, program, assessment result, or other product appears correct, polished, original, or high quality. Artifact quality does **not** prove that the human traversed the intended reasoning path. Conversely, structured learning gains can be real even when AI participates, so AI presence alone does not establish substitution.

### Cognitive effort / engagement
Task-level evidence that a participant is performing cognitive work. EEG/fMRI measures can be relevant, but lower connectivity or activation is not itself equivalent to lower intelligence, brain damage, reduced learning, or durable impairment.

### Recall
Ability to reproduce, quote, or recognize recently produced or learned material. Immediate task recall is distinct from long-term memory retention and transfer.

### Metacognition
Awareness and regulation of one's own reasoning. No current admitted empirical source in this topic establishes durable metacognitive change from LLM use.

### Dependence
A persistent inability or reduced willingness to perform a task without AI after prior AI use. A switch-condition effect is relevant but insufficient by itself to establish durable dependence.

### Independent reasoning / transfer
Ability to continue reasoning, discriminate evidence, solve novel problems, or transfer learning when AI assistance is unavailable or changed. This is closer to the topic's learning-transition question than terminal artifact quality alone, but it must be measured separately by task and timescale.

### Durable capability
Persistent change in cognitive or academic ability across time and preferably across tasks. No reviewed source in the topic currently establishes generalized durable capability loss from LLM use.

## Current evidence posture

The current evidence does **not** support a binary `AI use = cognitive harm` model.

Kosmyna et al. provide bounded experimental evidence that tool condition during essay writing can coincide with task-level differences in EEG connectivity, quotation/recall behavior, ownership, and essay characteristics. Their own institutional framing limits generalization, and the later Stankovic methodological comment adds material reasons for caution about sample size, reproducibility, EEG analysis, reporting, and transparency.

Peng et al. provide a materially different empirical lane: under a structured pre-lecture scaffolding design, AI interaction produced learning/neural-alignment benefits in the study framing and learning outcomes statistically comparable to human interaction. That does not prove all AI scaffolding is beneficial, but it is sufficient to reject a universal proposition that AI assistance necessarily substitutes for learning.

Pereira Campos & Koff reinforce the construct boundary rather than a harm finding: substitution and scaffolding must be distinguished, reduced neural engagement/connectivity is not synonymous with worse cognition, and long-term developmental effects remain an open empirical question.

The strongest current topic-level conclusion is therefore methodological: **AI interaction design, human cognitive responsibility, later unaided performance, and timescale must be represented separately.** Durable generalized cognitive decline remains unestablished.

## Required contradiction / null lanes

Before any reviewed topic finding is promoted, this matrix still requires meaningful evidence from these counterweights:

1. studies measuring **delayed unaided** reasoning, recall, or transfer after AI-supported learning;
2. null findings where prior AI assistance produces no later independent-performance difference;
3. replications or failures to replicate major neural/behavioral findings;
4. head-to-head answer-generation/substitution versus Socratic, critique, counterexample, tutoring, retrieval-practice, delayed-feedback, or answer-withholding interventions;
5. longitudinal evidence distinguishing temporary task-state effects from persistent capability change;
6. evidence across ages, subjects, task types, model families, and exposure durations;
7. validated metacognitive and dependence outcomes rather than inference from artifact quality or neural measures alone.

## Proposed analytical distinction

The topic retains `unobserved cognitive substitution` only as a proposed construct: a condition where AI performs cognitive transitions intended for the learner while the final artifact obscures that substitution. Current literature makes a scaffold-versus-substitute distinction plausible and empirically relevant, but the exact construct remains unvalidated until reviewed literature supports an operational definition, discriminant boundaries, and measurable positive/negative cases.
