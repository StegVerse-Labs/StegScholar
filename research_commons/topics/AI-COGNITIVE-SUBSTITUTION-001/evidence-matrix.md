## Construct boundaries

### Artifact quality
Whether the terminal essay, answer, proof, program, or other product appears correct, polished, original, or high quality. Artifact quality does **not** prove that the human traversed the intended reasoning path.

### Cognitive effort / engagement
Task-level evidence that a participant is performing cognitive work. EEG connectivity can be relevant, but lower connectivity is not itself equivalent to lower intelligence, brain damage, or durable impairment.

### Recall
Ability to reproduce, quote, or recognize recently produced or learned material. Immediate task recall is distinct from long-term memory retention.

### Metacognition
Awareness and regulation of one's own reasoning. No current source in this topic has yet been admitted as a direct metacognitive measure.

### Dependence
A persistent inability or reduced willingness to perform a task without AI after prior AI use. A switch-condition effect can be relevant but is insufficient by itself to establish durable dependence.

### Independent reasoning
Ability to continue reasoning when AI assistance is unavailable. This is closer to the topic's learning-transition question than terminal artifact quality alone, but it must be measured separately by task and timescale.

### Durable capability
Persistent change in cognitive or academic ability across time and preferably across tasks. No reviewed source in the topic currently establishes this construct.

## Current evidence posture

The MIT preprint provides the first admitted primary evidence for a narrower proposition: **under its essay-writing conditions, tool assignment was associated with experimentally observed differences in task-level neural connectivity, recall/quotation behavior, and essay characteristics.** It does not currently justify the stronger proposition that AI causes generalized or durable loss of thinking ability.

The institutional MIT project page itself limits generalization beyond the specific essay context, recommends larger and more diverse samples, notes EEG spatial-resolution limitations, and identifies longitudinal study as future work. Those limitations are treated as part of the evidence, not as optional caveats.

## Required contradiction / null lanes

Before any reviewed topic finding is promoted, this matrix must include evidence from at least these counterweights:

1. studies where AI-assisted learners match or outperform controls on later unaided reasoning or transfer tasks;
2. studies testing structured AI use that preserves or improves human reasoning transitions;
3. null findings for cognitive effort, recall, or independent performance;
4. interventions comparing answer-generation use with Socratic, critique, counterexample, tutoring, or delayed-feedback use;
5. longitudinal evidence distinguishing temporary task-state effects from persistent capability change;
6. evidence across age groups, subjects, task types, and multiple LLMs.

## Proposed analytical distinction

The topic retains `unobserved cognitive substitution` only as a proposed construct: a condition where AI performs cognitive transitions intended for the learner while the final artifact obscures that substitution. This construct remains unvalidated until reviewed literature supports measurable indicators and discriminant boundaries.
