## Session-unique conceptual framing

The following distinctions would be lost if the conversation were archived without durable capture:

1. **Capability produced is not the same as cognitive work performed.** A high-quality answer or artifact can coexist with little human reasoning during its production.
2. **The key risk hypothesis is unobserved cognitive substitution.** The relevant concern is not simply AI use, but a condition in which AI performs cognitive transitions that the learning process intended the human to traverse while the terminal artifact makes that substitution difficult to observe.
3. **Terminal-artifact evaluation is increasingly insufficient for learning provenance.** Assessing only the essay, proof, program, answer, or other final output may not establish that the learner acquired the intended capability.
4. **Learning may be represented as state transitions rather than only output quality.** The session proposed an illustrative sequence: `S0 vague intuition -> S1 proposition -> S2 conflicting evidence -> S3 discrimination -> S4 revised proposition -> S5 defensible conclusion`.
5. **AI can amplify rather than replace those transitions.** One candidate pattern is human proposition -> AI counterexample -> human discrimination -> AI alternative -> human reasoned acceptance/rejection -> revised human proposition.
6. **Governance/evaluation implication.** A future learning-integrity system should distinguish human-acquired state transitions, machine contribution, evidence provenance, and terminal artifact quality rather than treating those as interchangeable.

These are conceptual research hypotheses. They are not causal findings, validated educational metrics, or proof of cognitive decline.

## Evidence admission boundary

The session's prior prose referred to several studies and study summaries, but those references are **not admitted as reviewed evidence by this handoff** merely because they appeared in chat. Before promotion, the primary paper/preprint or authoritative record must be inspected and the following must be captured:

- study design and population;
- sample size and assignment method;
- AI condition and comparator condition;
- whether outcomes are self-reported, behavioral, physiological, academic, or longitudinal;
- what construct is actually measured: critical thinking, recall, cognitive effort, dependence, metacognition, neural activity, task accuracy, or another variable;
- causal versus correlational status;
- effect sizes and uncertainty where available;
- replication, external validity, and confounding limits;
- whether a result demonstrates temporary task-state differences versus durable capability change.

Secondary headlines or summaries must not be promoted into claims such as `AI causes loss of thinking ability` without evidence sufficient for that causal proposition.

## Unresolved risks and non-claims

- `lower task effort` is not automatically `loss of ability`;
- `different neural activation` is not automatically `brain damage`, `cognitive decline`, or durable impairment;
- better AI-assisted task performance is not automatically human learning;
- self-reported critical-thinking gains or dependence are not direct capability measurements;
- educational outcomes may differ by age, task, prompting style, motivation, subject, assessment design, and duration of AI exposure;
- the session's phrase `unobserved cognitive substitution` is a proposed analytical construct, not an established scientific term unless later literature review establishes prior use;
- the illustrative S0-S5 sequence is a modeling aid, not a validated psychometric scale.
