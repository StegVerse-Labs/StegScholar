## Canonical conceptual framing

1. **Capability produced is not the same as cognitive work performed.** A high-quality answer or artifact can coexist with little human reasoning during its production.
2. **The key risk hypothesis is unobserved cognitive substitution.** The concern is not simply AI use, but a condition in which AI performs cognitive transitions that the learning process intended the human to traverse while terminal artifact quality obscures that substitution.
3. **Terminal-artifact evaluation is insufficient by itself for learning provenance.** The final essay, proof, program, or answer does not establish that the learner acquired the intended capability.
4. **Learning may be represented as state transitions.** Illustrative modeling aid: `S0 vague intuition -> S1 proposition -> S2 conflicting evidence -> S3 discrimination -> S4 revised proposition -> S5 defensible conclusion`.
5. **AI can amplify rather than replace those transitions.** Candidate pattern: human proposition -> AI counterexample -> human discrimination -> AI alternative -> human reasoned acceptance/rejection -> revised human proposition.
6. **Interaction design is now an evidence-backed first-class variable.** Current reviewed evidence includes negative, null, and positive delayed outcomes depending on how AI support is structured; `AI access` alone is therefore an inadequate explanatory variable.

`unobserved cognitive substitution` and S0-S5 remain proposed analytical/modeling constructs, not established scientific terms or psychometric scales.

## Evidence admission boundary

Primary or authoritative research records must preserve:

- study design, population, sample size, and assignment method;
- AI condition and comparator condition;
- outcome type: self-report, behavioral, physiological, academic, transfer, or longitudinal;
- measured construct: artifact quality, recall, cognitive effort, metacognition, dependence, neural activity/connectivity, independent reasoning, transfer, or durable capability;
- causal versus correlational status;
- effect sizes and uncertainty where available;
- peer-review/version state;
- replication, external-validity, and confounding limits;
- task-state versus delayed/longitudinal interpretation;
- contradictory, null, and limitation evidence.

Secondary headlines cannot authorize claims such as `AI causes loss of thinking ability`. Lower neural activation/connectivity cannot be promoted as lower intelligence, brain damage, or durable impairment without construct-valid evidence.

## Reviewed evidence state

### ACS-SRC-002 — Kosmyna et al., `Your Brain on ChatGPT`, arXiv:2506.08872

Current authoritative/indexed posture remains **preprint**. Current verification found arXiv v2 revised 2025-12-31 and current bibliographic records still classify it as arXiv/CoRR; no journal successor was identified in the 2026-08-26 verification pass.

Bounded admitted facts include:

- 54 participants in sessions 1-3; 18 in optional session 4;
- individual random assignment across LLM, Search Engine, and Brain-only groups, balanced for age/gender according to the available primary/full-text record;
- GPT-4o used in the LLM condition;
- session-4 LLM-to-Brain and Brain-to-LLM switches;
- EEG connectivity, essay NLP, human/AI scoring, ownership interviews, and quotation recall;
- weaker reported EEG connectivity in the LLM condition and difficulty quoting produced work;
- reported reduced alpha/beta connectivity in the LLM-to-Brain session-4 condition.

Preserved limits: essay-writing context; no direct generalization to other LLMs; small optional session 4; EEG interpretation/spatial-resolution limits; no fine-grained task-substep decomposition; authors call for larger/diverse and longitudinal work. This is bounded task-level neural/behavioral evidence, not generalized durable cognitive-loss evidence.

### ACS-SRC-004 — Stankovic et al., arXiv:2601.00856

Methodological counter-comment. Preserved concerns include limited sample size, reproducibility, EEG analysis methodology, reporting consistency, and procedural/result transparency. Kosmyna et al. must therefore be interpreted with this counterweight rather than as a standalone settled finding.

### ACS-SRC-005 — Peng et al., Neuron 2026, DOI `10.1016/j.neuron.2026.04.005`

Peer-reviewed randomized structured-scaffolding evidence. Analyzed n=57 across no pre-lecture interaction, human pre-lecture interaction, and structured GPT-4-powered AI pre-lecture interaction. Outcomes included recall, comprehension, difficult-item comprehension, transfer, fMRI neural alignment, gaze alignment, and social closeness. The study reports learning/neural-alignment benefits in the structured AI condition and learning outcomes statistically comparable to the human interaction condition, while retaining some social/gaze differences. This contradicts a universal proposition that AI assistance necessarily reduces learning.

### ACS-SRC-006 — Pereira Campos & Koff, Frontiers in Developmental Psychology 2026

Peer-reviewed hypothesis/theory. It distinguishes cognitive debt, neural engagement, and neural connectivity; explicitly notes lower neural activity does not necessarily mean worse cognition; treats long-term adolescent executive-function effects as an unconfirmed hypothesis; and frames the important distinction as AI **substituting for** versus **scaffolding** cognitive/executive processes.

## Delayed / unaided evidence lane

Canonical machine file: `delayed-unaided-evidence.v1.json`, commit `0e81229a8febde49e4478145b6858c8d585e5ab0`.

### ACS-DU-001 — Barcaui 2025, Social Sciences & Humanities Open, DOI `10.1016/j.ssaho.2025.102287`

Peer-reviewed randomized trial, n=120. Undergraduates used unrestricted ChatGPT study aid or traditional non-AI study methods. A surprise retention test 45 days later reported 57.5% correct for the ChatGPT condition versus 68.5% for traditional study, `t(83)=-3.19`, `p=.002`, `d=.68`. This is meaningful bounded negative delayed-retention evidence. It does **not** by itself establish generalized cognitive decline or prove the proposed offloading mechanism.

### ACS-DU-002 — Georgetown medical-student RCT, 2025

Small prospective randomized trial comparing ChatGPT-4.0, external sources, and internal institutional materials. ChatGPT/external-resource groups performed better immediately, but a one-week repeat quiz without resources showed no significant group difference (`p=.118`). This is a required **null delayed-retention lane**, not evidence of durable benefit or harm.

### ACS-DU-003 — 2026 multisite cluster-randomized longitudinal GenAI feedback experiment

Peer-reviewed multisite field experiment with analytic n=1,176 across 48 course sections, four universities, and three science domains. Compared peer feedback, direct GenAI feedback, reflective GenAI feedback, and hybrid self-evaluation + peer + GenAI critique. Reflective/hybrid designs showed stronger feedback uptake/self-regulated learning and outperformed direct GenAI-supported feedback on **delayed AI-free transfer**. This is bounded positive evidence that learner-agency/scaffolding design can moderate later unaided performance.

## Unresolved risks and non-claims

- lower task effort is not automatically loss of ability;
- different neural activation/connectivity is not brain damage or durable impairment;
- immediate AI-assisted performance is not automatically learning;
- unrestricted-answer use and structured scaffolding are not equivalent interventions;
- immediate recall, delayed retention, near transfer, far transfer, metacognition, dependence, and durable capability are separate outcomes;
- positive structured-AI studies do not prove all AI use is beneficial;
- negative retention studies do not prove generalized de-skilling;
- no current evidence bundle supports a generalized population-level claim that LLM use causes durable cognitive decline;
- `unobserved cognitive substitution` remains proposed pending operational and independent validation.
