## Session-unique conceptual framing

1. **Capability produced is not the same as cognitive work performed.** A high-quality answer or artifact can coexist with little human reasoning during its production.
2. **The key risk hypothesis is unobserved cognitive substitution.** The relevant concern is not simply AI use, but a condition in which AI performs cognitive transitions that the learning process intended the human to traverse while the terminal artifact makes that substitution difficult to observe.
3. **Terminal-artifact evaluation is increasingly insufficient for learning provenance.** Assessing only the essay, proof, program, answer, or other final output may not establish that the learner acquired the intended capability.
4. **Learning may be represented as state transitions rather than only output quality.** The session proposed an illustrative sequence: `S0 vague intuition -> S1 proposition -> S2 conflicting evidence -> S3 discrimination -> S4 revised proposition -> S5 defensible conclusion`.
5. **AI can amplify rather than replace those transitions.** One candidate pattern is human proposition -> AI counterexample -> human discrimination -> AI alternative -> human reasoned acceptance/rejection -> revised human proposition.
6. **Governance/evaluation implication.** A future learning-integrity system should distinguish human-acquired state transitions, machine contribution, evidence provenance, and terminal artifact quality rather than treating those as interchangeable.

These are conceptual research hypotheses. They are not causal findings, validated educational metrics, or proof of cognitive decline.

## Evidence admission boundary

Before promotion, the primary paper/preprint or authoritative record must be inspected and the following must be captured:

- study design and population;
- sample size and assignment method;
- AI condition and comparator condition;
- whether outcomes are self-reported, behavioral, physiological, academic, or longitudinal;
- what construct is actually measured: critical thinking, recall, cognitive effort, dependence, metacognition, neural activity, task accuracy, or another variable;
- causal versus correlational status;
- effect sizes and uncertainty where available;
- replication, external validity, and confounding limits;
- whether a result demonstrates temporary task-state differences versus durable capability change.

Secondary headlines or summaries must not be promoted into claims such as `AI causes loss of thinking ability` without evidence sufficient for that causal proposition.

## 2026-08-26 machine-execution advancement

The discovery source was directly inspected. It relies materially on two linked evidence surfaces: a New York Times essay containing expert commentary and an MIT essay-writing study. The New York Times URL was identified, but its content was not directly reviewable through the current retrieval path and is therefore retained as secondary expert commentary only.

The first primary research record admitted into the topic is:

`Kosmyna et al., Your Brain on ChatGPT: Accumulation of Cognitive Debt when Using an AI Assistant for Essay Writing Task, arXiv:2506.08872 (2025)`.

The MIT Media Lab publication/project record and arXiv abstract were reviewed. Current admitted facts include:

- 54 participants completed sessions 1-3 and 18 completed session 4;
- experimental conditions were LLM, Search Engine, and Brain-only, with LLM-to-Brain and Brain-to-LLM switches in session 4;
- measures included EEG connectivity, essay NLP characteristics, human/AI scoring, ownership reports, and ability to quote produced work;
- the study reports the weakest EEG connectivity in the LLM group and stronger connectivity in Search Engine and Brain-only groups;
- the study reports difficulty accurately quoting produced essays among LLM users and reduced alpha/beta connectivity in the LLM-to-Brain switch condition;
- the authors' institutional project page explicitly limits generalization beyond the essay-writing context, notes that results cannot be directly generalized to other LLMs, identifies the small session-4 sample and EEG limitations, and calls for larger/diverse and longitudinal studies.

These findings are admitted only as bounded task-level neural/behavioral evidence. They do **not** establish that LLM use makes people dumber, causes brain damage, causes generalized cognitive decline, or produces durable capability loss.

## Unresolved risks and non-claims

- `lower task effort` is not automatically `loss of ability`;
- `different neural activation/connectivity` is not automatically `brain damage`, `cognitive decline`, lower intelligence, or durable impairment;
- better AI-assisted task performance is not automatically human learning;
- self-reported ownership, confidence, or dependence is not a direct capability measurement;
- immediate recall is distinct from durable retention and transfer;
- educational outcomes may differ by age, task, prompting style, motivation, subject, assessment design, AI interaction style, model, and duration of exposure;
- `unobserved cognitive substitution` remains a proposed analytical construct rather than an established scientific term;
- the illustrative S0-S5 sequence remains a modeling aid, not a validated psychometric scale;
- one preprint cannot support a topic-level causal conclusion.
