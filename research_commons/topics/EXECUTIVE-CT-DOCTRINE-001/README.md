# EXECUTIVE-CT-DOCTRINE-001

Research Commons topic for evidence-grounded analysis of the 2025–2026 U.S. Executive Branch counterterrorism doctrine, domestic `Antifa` / `Anti-Fascists` classifications, protest activity, and terrorism-related charging theories.

Canonical handoff: `EXECUTIVE_CT_DOCTRINE_MIRROR_HANDOFF.md`
Active issue: `StegVerse-Labs/StegScholar#54`

## Research questions

- What does the Executive Branch formally mean by `Antifa`, `Anti-Fascists`, `violent left-wing extremists`, and `domestic terrorist organization`?
- Which terms are legally defined, operationally defined, or left undefined?
- What authorities are invoked to investigate, disrupt, arrest, charge, sentence, or otherwise act against persons placed within these categories?
- In actual cases, which facts beyond political belief or peaceful protest supplied the statutory elements?
- Have peaceful protesters been arrested or charged under terrorism-related statutes, and if so, what exact conduct did the government allege?
- What constitutional limits govern ideology-, association-, speech-, protest-, or literature-based evidence?
- How do Executive Branch policy categories compare with narrower statutory definitions and judicial findings?

## Method

Primary legal and government sources first. Court documents outrank press characterizations for individual guilt or statutory elements. Government and defense narratives are retained separately. Claims are tracked at the defendant and count level where possible.

The lane deliberately searches for evidence that both supports and falsifies claims of ideological overbreadth. A peaceful protest occurring in the factual chronology does not by itself prove the protest was the criminal predicate; conversely, the absence of a standalone domestic-terrorism offense does not eliminate terrorism-related investigative or charging consequences.
