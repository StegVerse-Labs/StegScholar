# PESOSE: Track 1: Governed Open-Source Infrastructure for Recoverable AI Agent and Secure Communication Ecosystems

## Application

- Application ID: `FUNDING-NSF-PESOSE-2026-001`
- Solicitation: NSF 26-506
- Track: 1 — Scoping and planning
- Draft state: application process begun; not submission-ready
- Requested planning budget ceiling: USD 300,000

## Project overview

StegVerse is an emerging family of open-source protocols, reference implementations, validators, evidence formats, and governance controls designed to make AI-agent operations and secure digital communications more inspectable, recoverable, and resistant to silent authority drift. Existing work spans governed agents, runtime refusal, continuity receipts, admissibility checks, secure communications, provenance-preserving ingestion, deterministic validation, and cross-repository evidence custody.

The proposed Track 1 project will determine how these currently distributed research and engineering products can become a coherent, sustainable, secure open-source ecosystem. The planning effort will not claim established adoption or production maturity. It will produce tested ecosystem assumptions, a governance and licensing plan, contributor and user discovery evidence, a security/privacy risk plan, a sustainable managing-organization design, and a sequenced transition roadmap.

## National and societal need

AI agents are increasingly able to initiate or influence consequential actions across software, communications, finance, public services, and critical operations. Conventional logging and approval mechanisms often record that an action occurred without proving that execution authority remained valid, that policy and delegation were reconstructed at commit time, or that the system could recover as operator authority degraded.

StegVerse addresses this gap through open protocols and tools that distinguish approval from continuity, execution from admissibility, and replayability from reconstructability. A sustainable ecosystem could enable researchers, developers, public-interest organizations, companies, and governments to test and adopt interoperable controls for governed AI execution and secure communications without depending on one proprietary platform.

## Existing open-source product family

The proposal will identify one canonical public product as the required transition anchor and treat other StegVerse repositories as supporting components. Candidate anchor: `StegVerse-Labs/StegCore`, with supporting products including StegAgents, StegTalk, StegID, Continuity, StegScholar research artifacts, and ingestion/admissibility engines.

Before submission, the team must document:

1. public repository and license;
2. current development and testing model;
3. release and dissemination method;
4. verified users, contributors, forks, citations, pilots, or external implementations;
5. security posture and known limitations;
6. why an open-source ecosystem is preferable to a single-vendor product.

## Track 1 work plan

### 1. Ecosystem discovery

- Interview prospective users and contributors across AI safety, cybersecurity, digital rights, research infrastructure, secure communications, and regulated operations.
- Test demand for the canonical protocols and determine which artifacts are sufficiently mature to anchor an ecosystem.
- Map current and prospective adopters, maintainers, validators, educators, institutional partners, and downstream integrators.
- Produce falsifiable adoption hypotheses and evidence-backed go/no-go criteria for a later Track 2 effort.

### 2. Organization and governance

- Design a managing-organization model that separates research authority, implementation authority, validation authority, financial authority, publication authority, and protected IP custody.
- Define contributor roles, decision rights, refusal and appeal mechanisms, release authority, conflict handling, succession, and maintainer continuity.
- Evaluate licensing and contributor-agreement options while preserving open interoperability and avoiding accidental disclosure of protected inventions.
- Establish transparent roadmapping and durable task-claim controls that prevent duplicate or conflicting execution.

### 3. Risk analysis, safety, security, and privacy

- Threat-model code, models, workflows, registries, ingestion bundles, validators, and supply-chain dependencies.
- Define identity, signing, chain-of-custody, provenance, artifact hashing, and release-verification requirements.
- Evaluate privacy risks in secure communications and evidence retention.
- Define incident response, rollback, recovery, compromised-maintainer, poisoned-model, and dependency-failure procedures.
- Align planning with applicable CISA, NSA, and OpenSSF secure-development guidance.

### 4. Community building

- Conduct governed workshops and structured discovery sessions.
- Publish contributor pathways, technical onboarding, validation challenges, and reference implementations.
- Develop multilingual onboarding beginning with English, Spanish, Simplified Chinese, and Traditional Chinese.
- Create pathways for academic, industry, government, and independent researchers to contribute without receiving unbounded execution authority.

### 5. Sustainability and transition roadmap

- Develop funding, sponsorship, service, training, certification, and public-good support scenarios.
- Define measurable ecosystem health, security, contribution, adoption, and governance indicators.
- Produce a staged roadmap from Track 1 planning through ecosystem formation, security hardening, and independent validation.

## Planned deliverables

1. Ecosystem discovery report and stakeholder map.
2. Canonical-product and supporting-component maturity assessment.

## Intellectual merit

The project investigates whether explicit continuity, admissibility, reconstructability, and recoverable-authority mechanisms can form a practical interoperability layer for open AI-agent and secure-communication ecosystems. It also tests governance structures that bind execution to inspectable evidence rather than relying only on organizational intent or static approval.

## Broader impacts

A successful planning project could lower barriers to trustworthy AI and secure communications research, improve open-source security and maintainer continuity, create educational and workforce pathways, and help public and private organizations evaluate agentic systems with clearer evidence and authority boundaries.
