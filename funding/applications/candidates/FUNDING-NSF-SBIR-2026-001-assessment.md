# NSF SBIR/STTR 2026 StegVerse Assessment

## Candidate ID

`FUNDING-NSF-SBIR-2026-001`

## Opportunity fit

The NSF Seed Fund Artificial Intelligence and Cybersecurity and Authentication topics are plausible commercialization pathways for several StegVerse capabilities:

- StegCore and governed AI-agent execution;
- StegAgents interoperability and runtime controls;
- StegTalk secure machine-to-machine communications;
- StegID device and personal authentication;
- continuity, admissibility and evidence-verification tooling;
- privacy-preserving, explainable and secure AI infrastructure.

The AI topic explicitly includes trustworthy AI that is safe, fair, transparent, privacy-preserving, explainable or secure. The Cybersecurity and Authentication topic includes data privacy and integrity, device authentication, trusted computing and secure machine-to-machine communication.

## Commercial project candidates

### Candidate A — Governed AI execution gateway

A deployable engine that evaluates agent actions against policy, delegation, evidence and commit-time validity before execution, with deterministic denial and reconstruction receipts.

Primary repositories: StegCore, StegAgents, Continuity and StegID.

Commercial hypothesis: organizations adopting agentic systems need an independent execution-control and audit layer that can enforce authority boundaries across model and vendor changes.

### Candidate B — Secure resilient communications stack

A multi-bearer secure communications system with device pairing, identity verification, resilient transport, privacy-preserving delivery evidence and governed recovery.

Primary repositories: StegTalk, StegID and related secure-communications components.

Commercial hypothesis: regulated, remote, contested and intermittently connected operations need communications that can preserve integrity and controlled recovery across multiple transports.

### Candidate C — Evidence continuity and provenance verifier

A verification service and SDK for validating provenance, transition admissibility, reconstructability, supply-chain evidence and AI-ready data integrity.

Primary repositories: Data-Continuation, master-records, StegScholar and ingestion engines.

Commercial hypothesis: scientific, regulated and AI-enabled workflows need machine-verifiable evidence continuity rather than conventional logs alone.

## Mandatory eligibility gates

No Project Pitch or proposal should be represented as applicant-ready until evidence establishes:

1. a U.S.-located small business with fewer than 500 employees;
2. at least 50% qualifying U.S.-citizen or permanent-resident equity ownership;
3. funded work performed in the United States;
4. a PI legally employed at least 20 hours weekly by the applicant;
5. PI commitment meeting NSF effort requirements;
6. applicant control of or licensed rights to the proposed technology;
7. a technically risky innovation rather than ordinary product development;
8. a credible commercial market and customer-discovery pathway;
9. disclosure classification and freedom-to-operate review;
10. applicant registrations and submission authority.

## Evidence required before selecting a candidate

- exact legal applicant and capitalization record;
- current repository licenses and IP ownership chain;
- implemented product boundary and test evidence;
- technical-risk statement and measurable Phase I feasibility objectives;
- customer and competitor evidence;
- market size and commercialization pathway;
- PI qualifications and employment evidence;
- budget assumptions;
- protected-disclosure classification.
